Junior AP additional/replacement liens and administrative claims require specified senior priority; cash AP remains objectionable. Pay-over is limited to the lesser of junior receipts and senior cash shortfall, with the prescribed equal-value property exchange (ICA pp.7–8 L349–L373).

Ordinary dispositions require senior permission and no express junior prohibition. A cash bid buys **Collateral**, not senior debt; junior credit bidding requires cash sufficient to cause senior discharge. ### Recovery intake conclusion

The positive finding is contractual access asymmetry, not a quantified loss. Conditionally, declaration/demand and receipt initiate restrictions; loss of unilateral realization timing is the first legal break. If assets deteriorate during delay, senior accruals or qualifying financing could absorb more proceeds; a conditioned sale/release could replace guarantee/asset access with residual proceeds. Observable tests are notices, diligence/stay filings, claims, valuations, DIP/AP orders, sale consideration and discharge records—not assumed issuer events.

The strongest countercase combines reciprocal grants and matching-collateral intent, residual proceeds, qualified payments, conditional bidding/AP rights and anti-disproportionate-amendment safeguards (§§2.3–2.4,5.3). It wins economically if verified coextensive perfected coverage, immaterial exceptions and ample net value after actual senior claims are demonstrated. Control asymmetry alone cannot defeat that case or establish the fulcrum.
