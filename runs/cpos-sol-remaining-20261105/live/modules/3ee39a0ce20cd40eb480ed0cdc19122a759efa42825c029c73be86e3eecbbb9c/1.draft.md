### Intercreditor conclusion

**Evidence → control → consequence:** LMI's executed-text ICA supports a positive, provision-level conclusion that junior collateral access is substantially dependent on senior action; missing economics do not erase that finding. Conversely, the nineteen acknowledgers do not establish where value sits, and senior control does not prove low recovery. The strongest protective case combines reciprocal grant discipline and matching-collateral intent with residual proceeds, ordinary payments, conditional junior bidding, AP rights and anti-disproportionate-amendment protections (T4F.2–5). It would change the economic downside conclusion if schedules and valuations showed materially coextensive perfected reach, immaterial equity exceptions and ample value after actual all-in senior claims. It cannot eliminate the documented control restrictions.

Time-aware downside is conditional, not issuer chronology: default declaration plus demand and receipt start the junior waiting analysis; first legal break is lost unilateral collateral timing. If operations/asset quality then deteriorate, senior interest/fees and qualifying financing could consume more value during delay; a senior-led sale can release asset/guarantee access and refinancing can continue priority. The effect is access and potential LGD/refinancing exposure, not proved deterioration, liquidity exhaustion or higher PD. Observable tests are received declarations/demands/notices, material-collateral enforcement diligence and stay filings, valuations, claims, DIP orders, sale proceeds/releases and effective-date discharge evidence (ICA §§3.1,4,5.1,5.6,6; locators in registers). No actual default or proceeding is inferred.
