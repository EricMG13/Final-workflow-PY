## Findings

**Assessment — Analyst Interpretation; confidence Medium: TD Group's installed-base component franchise and improving historical operating cash generation mitigate business risk, but debt-funded shareholder distributions and rising carrying leverage limit creditor benefit from those earnings; retained liquidity, forward refinancing capacity and legal headroom are not established. ### Committee findings in priority order

1. **Cash retention, not just earnings resilience, is the central credit distinction.** CP-1A BF01/X04 establishes a direct September borrowing-to-dividend funding link; CP-1 BS14 and SE03 distinguish the accrued obligation from October payment. Debt-funded equity transfers leave fixed claims without an acquired operating earnings stream. This raises leverage-tolerance and extraction risk even when operations improve; it is not a finding of unlawful payment, priming or coercive LME.
2. **The franchise offers a credible, conditional business-risk mitigant.** CP-1A B03/B04 describes estimated proprietary/aftermarket content and historically stronger aftermarket economics; B10 ties demand to utilization and fleet conditions. Replacement consumption can be more persistent than new aircraft deliveries, supporting margin and cash generation. It is not contracted recurring revenue, verified sole-source share or a cash-flow floor.
3. **Historical financial progress did not translate into lower gross carrying leverage.** CP-1 K05/K06/K12 shows improving operating performance and cash conversion, while K09 increases between FY2023 and FY2024. A lower mechanical cash-netted multiple is not equivalent to stronger retained liquidity because cash preceded a declared payment and accessibility is unproven.
4. **Historical refinancing actions are useful counterevidence, not forward clearance.** CP-1A X06/BF02 documents extensions and repricing. These mitigated selected historical funding burdens but did not retire consolidated debt; secured substitution also changes the claims mix. Instrument-level reconciliation and dated execution evidence belong with CP-3C, and legal consequences with CP-4.

### Business, ownership and operating economics

**Structure and control.** TD Group is the listed holding company; TransDigm Inc. is its wholly-owned operating subsidiary and operating debt issuer. TD Group and other subsidiaries have guarantor roles in the reported 2033 financing. Power & Control, Airframe and Non-aviation are reporting segments, not legal obligors. Consequently, consolidated earnings, reported cash and segment assets do not identify cash available at a particular issuer or collateral available to a particular creditor. Basis: CP-1 T4.2 and CP-1A B01/B02/O01. Beneficial ownership, voting control, board independence, affiliate arrangements and sponsor identity/support are Not supplied in the necessary detail; no controlling sponsor or support willingness is inferred from a public listing (O02–O04, GT04/GT09).

**Revenue durability and competition.** Management estimates approximately 55% of FY2024 sales came from aftermarket and approximately 90% from proprietary products; it characterizes aftermarket revenues as historically higher gross profit and more stable than OEM sales. These remain **Management Language**, not independently established competitive statistics. Long aircraft/product lives and qualification investments can slow switching and sustain replacement demand, creating pricing discretion and earnings visibility. However, a proprietary design is not automatically sole-source, and installed-base opportunity is not backlog. Predominantly point-in-time recognition makes shipments and control transfer relevant to realized revenue. Basis: CP-1A B03/B04/B08/B09 and CT01; inherited S1 pp.4, 7, 9, 120–121.

**Price, volume and mix.** Value-based pricing is management's stated approach, not a quantified FY2024 price contribution. Utilization, fleet age/size, airline economics, OEM production schedules and defense budgets are supported volume mechanisms (B09/B10). Pricing can offset cost pressure only if customers accept increases and regulatory/contract conditions permit them. OEM/aftermarket and defense mix can change consolidated margins even when total sales rise. The observed annual sales increase must not be labeled wholly organic, price-driven or volume-driven: acquired contributions and a checked price/volume bridge are Not supplied (CP-1A X01/X02/G07).

**Diversification with correlated exposures.** Multiple component families, direct customers, distributors and destination markets broaden demand sources (B02/B05–B07). Note-level customer disclosure supports the absence of an individually dominant customer at the stated reporting threshold; management also reports meaningful top-customer concentration. Such breadth reduces isolated counterparty risk, but commercial aerospace utilization, OEM production and government spending remain common drivers. Distributors can shift stocking ahead of end-user consumption. Destination sales do not reveal functional-currency exposure, cash location or accessibility. This supports diversification as a mitigant, not immunity from sector stress.

**Costs and capex.** Product teams, decentralized manufacturing and investment in tooling/automation provide potential productivity levers (B11/B12). Conversely, qualified supply chains, specialized labor and certification constraints impede rapid substitutions and can generate expediting, idle-capacity or inventory costs. Historical signed capex is modest relative to operating cash, but the maintenance/growth split and fixed/variable cost structure are Not supplied. Therefore, capex flexibility is bounded: reducing investment cannot be assumed costless to reliability, certification or future competitiveness. Acquisitions are separate cash uses, not capex within CP-1 K02.

**Cash conversion.** CP-1 CF22, K03 and K12 show stronger operating cash generation and a smaller annual cash use in the complete changes-in-assets/liabilities block in FY2024. Inventory, receivables and unbilled performance can still absorb cash before collection; advance receipts provide partial offsets (CP-1A B13/CT02). Interest, taxes, integration costs, acquisition spend and distributions prevent adjusted EBITDA or annual CFO less capex from representing freely distributable cash. CP-1's ordinary EBITDA and EBITDA As Defined are different reported non-GAAP measures; neither is automatically accepted economic earnings or legal EBITDA.

### Historical change and limits

FY2024 operating results improved from FY2023: reported revenue increased from USD 6,585 million to USD 7,940 million, and operating income from USD 2,923 million to USD 3,531 million (CP-1 IS01/IS06; S1 p.106). The checked CP-1 K13 deltas are USD 1,355 million and USD 608 million, respectively. EBITDA As Defined increased from USD 3,395 million to USD 4,173 million, while its calculated margin fraction increased from 0.515566 to 0.525567 (SG16/K06). This establishes historical earnings progress, not a verified organic or price-led bridge. CP-1A X01/X02 documents acquisitions in the same period; their full-year contribution and realized integration returns are not established.

Operating cash increased from USD 1,375 million to USD 2,045 million; CP-1 calculated CFO less signed capex increased from USD 1,236 million to USD 1,880 million (CF22/CF23/K02). Conversion to EBITDA As Defined improved from 0.405007 to 0.490055 (K12). The complete annual assets/liabilities cash effect remained negative, at USD (431) million in FY2023 and USD (302) million in FY2024 (K03). Improvement therefore coexisted with continuing cash absorption, and unresolved cash-bridge scope differences prevent collapsing all change measures into one working-capital definition.

The financing comparison is less favorable for retention. Carrying debt increased from USD 19,750 million to USD 24,880 million, and carrying debt/EBITDA As Defined increased from 5.817378 to 5.962138 multiples (K07/K09). Cash rose from USD 3,472 million to USD 6,261 million, producing a lower mechanical cash-netted ratio, but FY2024 ended with USD 4,216 million of dividends payable (BS01/BS14/K10). CP-1A BF01 supplies the direct debt-funded distribution link; X06 supplies the countervailing history of successful extensions. October payments are subsequent events, not FY2024 cash outflows. These observations explain why improved operations and a larger cash balance do not establish deleveraging or a permanently stronger liquidity buffer.

### Ranked strengths, weaknesses and key risks

**Strengths**

1. **Installed-base replacement economics — Analyst Interpretation.** CP-1A B03/B04/B09 supports a conditional durability thesis: long-lived qualified components and proprietary-content estimates create repeat replacement demand and potential pricing discretion, which can stabilize debt-service earnings relative to reliance solely on new aircraft production. Credible counterevidence is utilization sensitivity, government remedies and supply constraints (B10/B11/B14). The view would strengthen with verified platform/aftermarket profitability and stable end-user orders; it would weaken with sustained substitution, qualification loss, adverse pricing acceptance or utilization-driven demand erosion.

2. **Improving historical earnings and cash conversion — Analyst Interpretation.** CP-1 IS06, K04–K06 and K12 show stronger annual profitability and conversion, while CF22/CF23 supports positive CFO less capex. Greater operating cash generation improves the ability to meet cash obligations before capital allocation. Counterevidence is continuing working-capital cash use, acquisition/integration demands and untested add-backs (K03, CF24, EB06–EB09; CP-1A CT02). Consistent cash collections, evidenced maintenance needs and a challenged earnings-to-cash bridge would strengthen this view; margin-to-cash divergence would weaken it.

3. **Breadth and documented historical financing execution — Analyst Interpretation.** CP-1A B02/B05–B07 and X06/BF02 support product/customer breadth and completed tenor extensions. These reduce isolated operating exposures and selected historical funding pressure. Common sector drivers and secured substitution remain counterevidence, while missing market inputs prevent extrapolating access. Better exposure detail and verified refinancing terms would improve confidence; concentrated order deterioration or materially more restrictive financing would reduce it.

**Weaknesses**

1. **Debt-funded extraction limits retained creditor cushion — Analyst Interpretation; Material risk.** CP-1A BF01/GT01/GT02 and CP-1 BS14 establish borrowing linked to a non-earning equity payout and a substantial accrued payment. The mechanism leaves debt service behind while removing cash, restricting the inference from gross base-date liquidity and elevating future capital-allocation monitoring. No FY2024/FY2023 buybacks, explicit reporting and historical extensions are credible contrary evidence, not reversal of the dividend. Sustained retention, restrained distributions and evidenced debt reduction would improve the view; further debt-funded shareholder returns would weaken it.

2. **Leverage leaves less tolerance for earnings or financing setbacks — Analyst Interpretation; Material risk.** CP-1 K07/K09 shows carrying debt and carrying leverage increased despite stronger earnings. In the downside chain, lower earnings or new borrowing would increase the burden on retained cash and refinancing. Improved operating-income/net-interest coverage (K11) is counterevidence, but is neither cash coverage nor covenant headroom. Accepted earnings, sustained cash retention and verified debt retirement would improve the view; persistent cash-funded shortfalls or incremental debt without contribution would weaken it.

3. **Acquisitions create competing cash demands and integration exposure — Analyst Interpretation; Material risk.** CP-1A X01/X02/GT03 and CP-1 CF24 show repeated acquisitions, including debt-linked CPI funding. New businesses may add earnings and diversification, but cash integration, preliminary allocations and uncertain contribution can postpone creditor benefit. Multiple added product exposures are genuine counterevidence to a purely adverse acquisition view. Demonstrated acquired cash returns and successful controls integration would improve it; acquisition-driven debt growth without realized contribution would weaken it.

4. **Sector and qualified-supply sensitivity can transmit into cash quickly — Analyst Interpretation; Material risk.** CP-1A B07/B10/B11/B14 supports utilization, OEM, budget, distributor and certification dependencies. Correlated volume declines or production constraints can reduce shipments, pressure mix and absorb inventory cash, weakening liquidity before management can fully adjust costs. Aftermarket content and customer/product breadth mitigate this mechanism. Stable end-use demand, qualified supply and collections would support the view; persistent order cancellations, inventory accumulation, labor disruption or contract remedies would weaken it. No current adverse event is inferred.

The ranking reflects supported mechanics, not numerical risk probabilities. Missing liquidity, market, ownership and legal inputs are separately recorded as gaps, not additional adverse behavior. CP-1A's bounded governance rubric is not reproduced as a CP-2 composite rating or independently rescored.
