### Funding conclusion

CP-2H's rating distinctions materially constrain reliance on CP-2G. S&P's issuer and proposed unsecured-note ratings were preliminary BB-/Positive; finalisation required successful issuance, satisfactory final documentation and operating performance consistent with its base case. Material departures could lead to revision or withdrawal. Fitch's BB+/Stable issuer rating was published, but its proposed-note BB+(EXP)/RR4 remained expected and conditional on refinancing completion and conforming documents. Neither rating status proves settlement. Their two-symbol-step difference establishes neither a pricing advantage nor default/LME intent. (S pp.1,3 L173–182; F pp.1–2 L5–8,153–166.)

The proposed up-to-USD500 million parent unsecured bond serves general corporate purposes, including strategic capex and refinancing short debt. The EUR435 million Romanian subsidiary facility was signed in March 2024 for capex. These are distinct currencies, borrowers and purposes; the TRY amounts are S&P's approximate translations, not this module's FX conversions. Signing alone does not prove unconditional availability, upstream transferability or eligibility to meet parent maturities. Draw conditions, eligible expenditure, use controls and local covenant compliance must be checked. (S p.1 L173–179; M p.2 L475–517.)

The single-source cases retain nominal coverage but little room for weaker cash FFO, a delayed draw or intra-year mismatch. The combined deficit identifies the amount of unresolved annual funding need under those assumptions; it does not identify which payment would fail first. An annual surplus cannot establish daily cash adequacy. S&P identifies short maturities and absence of a long-term committed RCF as limitations. Fitch's EUR100 million working-capital revolver and expected rolling short-term renewals are supportive, but cannot be added as unverified incremental cash. (M p.2 L479–499; F p.2 L126–135.)

CP-2G's improving leverage ratio is earnings-led, not debt repayment: its adjusted debt rises across both forecast years. CP-2H also preserves Fitch's approximate around-2x net-leverage outlook versus the strict above-2.0x-by-2025 sensitivity; an exact breach or buffer is not established. S&P gross leverage cannot fill that gap. Romanian hard-currency cash matters separately: reported earnings growth will not protect creditors if collections are unusable for foreign debt service and imports. (Supplied CP-2G/CP-2H; S p.3 L184–202; F pp.1–2 L94–124.)

Fitch's April 2024 expectation places most term loan Bs and the planned bond in a conditional 2026–28 maturity cluster after refinancing. This would ease nearer-term needs while concentrating later refinancing risk; it is not a complete maturity wall or an upcoming-2026 monitoring assertion made with later knowledge. Exact dates, amortisation and executed tenor are Not supplied. (F p.2 L133–135.)

The strongest contrary case is ordinary investment-cycle funding supported by contractual volumes, cost-plus expense recovery and capex recovery over the product cycle, rapid hard-currency collections and strategic importance to FMC. Dividend reductions have historical precedent; spending flexibility may bridge the investment trough. This countercase wins if closing evidence and executable draws arrive before uses, protected volumes become collected usable cash, Romanian ramp economics hold, and distributions/spending adjust before buffers disappear. Contract protection is not a corporate debt guarantee: Fitch notes absent debt guarantees/cross-default and does not apply its potential parent uplift. (M p.1 L24–39,51–80; S p.1 L135–158.)
