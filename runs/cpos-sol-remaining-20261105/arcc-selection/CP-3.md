## Findings

### Fundamental credit context

ARCC is an externally managed BDC investing mainly in middle-market debt and equity, not an industrial borrower. Annual A3 and A94 describe investment funding and swaps intended to align fixed-issued debt with a primarily floating-rate loan portfolio. Thus matched assets/liabilities, asset performance, liquidity access and valuation matter more than an invented industrial EBITDA multiple. Manager affiliation supplies operating context, not a committed support obligation. Substantially all investments lack readily available quotations; A96/A99 explicitly warn that forced realizations can be materially below reported values. That uncertainty affects the assets supporting these unsecured claims, not merely accounting volatility.

The source-backed upstream fundamental view is mixed, not blanket deterioration. A116 shows investment income 2990 versus 2614 USDm and NII 1403 versus 1266; net operations are 1522 in both years, while diluted EPS is 2.44 versus 2.68. Higher NII is a substantive debt-service countercase, but aggregate growth does not establish recurring cash realization or per-share growth. A73 nonaccruals rose from 1.3% to 1.7% at amortized cost and 0.6% to 1.0% at fair value; average internal grade remained 3.1. Grade 4 is the issuer's best grade, opposite the CP-3 risk-score direction; 3.1 is not imported as a CP-3 score. The two quality signals must coexist.

A255 CFO of -2128 versus +511 USDm includes purchases -12622 and sales/repayments 9212, plus PIK interest/dividend adjustments -206/-257 and collections +109/+33 in 2024. These are portfolio deployment and realization flows, not industrial cash burn or a CFO-minus-capex FCF measure. A115 ordinary cash 635 is distinct from restricted cash 225 and cash-flow ending combined cash 860. Debt caption 13727 and separate Secured borrowings 32 remain separate; neither is silently redefined. A255 distributions paid 1139 and common issuance proceeds 1364 show cash outflows and new capital together, not a debt-funded recap or assured equity backstop. A89 reports no open-market program repurchases in 2024/2023. Governance concerns here are valuation incentives and cash-retention trade-offs; neither managerial support nor adverse conduct is presumed.

C's current July finding of published 196.2% coverage versus 150% supports a historical financing-flexibility countercase; it is not freely drawable cash or dollar capacity. The facility's separate maintenance, equity and borrowing-base requirements can constrain access before payment default, and C does not supply its actual tested ratio. A88's approximately 5.1bn conditional facility availability cannot simply be added to cash without testing restrictions and uses. The nearest supported pressure point is weaker borrower performance/marks reducing coverage and collateral eligibility; funding access could then tighten, forcing more reliance on collections, asset sales or equity before refinancing dated parent maturities. This is a conditional causal pathway, not a forecast of default. The initiating observables are nonaccruals, realization versus marks, borrowing-base reports, certificates, actual draws and executed returns.

The strongest issuer-level contrary case is higher NII, unchanged average grade, positive published coverage, dated issuer compliance assertions and subsequent January 2025 market issuance. It wins against immediate funding-closure claims if transaction-date certificates demonstrate surviving pro forma Act coverage, facility maintenance/equity/base tests, lender commitments and accessible cash after uses. Those inputs are not supplied. Therefore conviction is higher in the historical facts than in future liquidity sufficiency. No borrower-level valuation, complete funding forecast, ratings/trigger review or governing factor weights supports a composite or rating tier.
