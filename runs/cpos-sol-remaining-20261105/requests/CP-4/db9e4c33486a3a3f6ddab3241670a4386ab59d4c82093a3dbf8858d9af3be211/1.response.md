## Findings

### Legal assessment

**The supplied 2033 indenture combines meaningful payment, lien and voting protections with aggressive borrower flexibility in distributions, investments and legal earnings adjustments.** Its principal weakness is not a demonstrated covenant breach: it is the ability to remove or relocate value while satisfying transaction-based tests, including an unrestricted-payment route when legal fixed-charge coverage exceeds 2.0x. The strongest contrary evidence is the express junior-lien-or-Permitted-Lien rule, joint-and-several payment guarantees, affected-holder protection for ranking/payment changes and a two-thirds collateral/ICA consent threshold. Those protections can constrain an overt priority rewrite, but they do not eliminate expressly permitted leakage, releases or pari dilution. Exact capacity, perfection and enforcement control remain unproven without the missing package and transaction inputs.

This assessment is **Analyst Interpretation**, based on clause-level Documentary Facts below. It is instrument-specific, not a finding that all TransDigm debt has identical terms. Provisional covenant-aggressiveness assessment is **4/5, Aggressive**, not a market-relative grade. Confidence is Medium for credit implications and higher for the directly read wording; package-wide execution/compliance conclusions are not supported.

### Legal risks and protections

**Definitions change capacity, not just reported leverage.** L p.5 L410–L424 defines FCCR as legal EBITDA over Fixed Charges for four full quarters with internal statements, pro forma financing and acquisitions/dispositions, transaction-date floating rates and swap effects. Fixed Charges adds tax-grossed preferred dividends to defined interest. Defined interest excludes financing-fee amortization, bridge/commitment fees, qualified securitization costs and prepayment premiums, while adding capitalized interest and capital-lease interest. EBITDA includes expansive transaction, restructuring, dividend-equivalent and projected-savings adjustments. Clause M's 35% limit is neither a global cap on all EBITDA add-backs nor a cap on every pro forma acquisition adjustment: L421–L422 separately permits officer-good-faith acquisition calculations and reasonably determined operating expense reductions. The no-duplication and specified exclusions still matter; the document is not permission to add the same amount twice. Wider earnings and narrower fixed charges can make ratio debt and distributions easier before actual cash savings arrive, increasing debt-service sensitivity and weakening the residual asset cushion. Monitor adjustment categories, actual realization and transaction-date rate inputs, not the accounting multiple.

**Debt and lien permissions are separate.** L p.18 §4.09 L1295 says **“greater than 2.0 to 1.0”**, not greater than or equal. L p.10 L715 says **“shall not exceed 5.25 to 1.00”** for the specific (xxxi) Ratio Indebtedness lien route. Permitted Liens (iii) covers Bank Indebtedness; (xx) links purchase-money and additional-debt baskets to asset-specific/collateral-only limits; (xxi) confines foreign-sub liens to foreign-sub assets. Accordingly, 5.25 is not a blanket secured leverage maintenance ceiling or a condition on every lien. Permitted-debt reclassification can refresh fixed baskets; acquisition debt can use an improved-FCCR alternative even without satisfying the USD1 ratio-debt test. These provisions can permit further pari claims or locally senior funding without a new breach, but actual priming is not established. The contrary case is §4.12's express junior-or-permitted requirement and §9.02's affected-holder ranking protection. A proposed priority change must satisfy those boundaries and the missing ICA, rather than relying on a generic majority-vote claim.

**Leakage can occur before default and may outlast deteriorating tests.** §4.07 L1265 permits **“any Restricted Payment”** subject to no continuing Default/EoD and pro forma FCCR exceeding 2.0x. L1272 preserves certain payments contractually committed while conditions held. The builder basket therefore is not the sole potential distribution constraint. Permitted Investments can reach non-guarantors, and designation of a guarantor as Unrestricted can release its guarantee and liens under §§10.07(c),12.07(f), subject to designation and RP conditions. Asset-sale exclusions, §5.01's intragroup carveout and affiliate carveouts mean a transfer may avoid one covenant yet remain subject to others. No categorical IP-transfer prohibition is identified in the supplied indenture; this is a conditional route, not a finding that material IP has been transferred. Without an entity/IP schedule, valuation and usage ledger its amount is Not calculable. These mechanics can reduce creditors' reachable value without an immediate payment default; monitor designations, intragroup transfers, RP commitments and releases as well as reported dividends.

**The historical distribution demonstrates willingness, not a capacity opinion.** S1 p.131 expressly links September notes and Tranche L proceeds plus existing cash to the USD75/share declaration and eligible option equivalents. S1 p.146 records USD4,216 million common dividends accrued at September 30 and USD4,216 million common plus USD132 million equivalent payments in **October 2024**. The latter are subsequent cash uses, not FY2024 cash flow. Neither the funding link nor the issuer compliance statement on p.132 identifies the precise §4.07 route, verifies conditions at declaration/commitment or proves residual capacity. A general compliance assertion is not the missing transaction certificate. Retain CP-1A BF01 as extraction behavior, not unlawful payment or coercive LME evidence.

**Asset-sale protection is real but delayed and qualified.** FMV consideration and conditional cash requirements, reinvestment/application deadlines, and a pro rata 100%-plus-interest offer protect noteholders where §4.10 applies. Countervailing flexibility includes deemed cash (assumed liabilities, instruments converted within 180 days and designated noncash consideration), long reinvestment periods, net-proceeds deductions, use to repay secured or non-guarantor debt, and return of untendered residual to permitted general purposes. The special productive-assets alternative requires substitute Productive Assets promptly to become Collateral where sold assets were collateral (L1317). This supports collateral continuity in that path, but is not a universal requirement that all proceeds immediately repay these Notes. Do not collapse asset-sales proceeds into near-term note amortization.

**Guarantees improve direct recourse but are neither universal nor permanent.** §10.01 promises unconditional, irrevocable, joint-and-several payment/performance, not merely collection, with reinstatement for rescinded payments; §10.02 limits enforceability by fraudulent-transfer law. The executed signer list includes CPI, Calspan, Raptor entities and TransDigm UK, among others (L pp.31–32), but is not a current exhaustive ownership/value schedule. §4.17's facility limb and separate >=200 capital-markets limb can capture new obligors, with 20-Business-Day accession; §12.04 links required guarantee accession to security/ICA joinder. §10.07 nonetheless releases on permitted external disposals, compliant USub designation, immateriality under its conditions, release from specified triggering debt, or funded discharge/defeasance. Immaterial Domestic Restricted Subsidiary requires both <=2.5% EBITDA and <=2.5% assets, using the specified >45-day reporting lag (L p.7 L535); no aggregate immateriality cap is stated in that definition. A non-guarantor is not the same as an Unrestricted Subsidiary, and a foreign subsidiary is not automatically outside every guarantee: TransDigm UK is expressly excluded from the defined Foreign Restricted Subsidiary category and signs here.

**Security documentation determines realized control.** Collateral excludes the absent Security Agreement's Excluded Collateral (L p.4 L389). §12.03 conditions some further collateralization on corresponding facility requirements; §12.06 provides commercially reasonable perfection efforts and a 90-day outside period, not evidence those filings were completed. §12.07(e)(B) follows specified Credit Agreement Collateral Agent releases, but excludes the stated full-cash facility discharge outside foreclosure/remedies; it is not an automatic release whenever the facility is repaid. §12.07(e)(A) can release collateral transferred outside Company/Holdings/Guarantors in a permitted transaction, potentially including a restricted non-guarantor recipient, subject to full operative conditions. Permitted automatic releases differ from amendments releasing substantially all collateral outside the documents. Certificates and opinions are required to evidence requested releases; none is supplied here.

**LME/control protection is mixed rather than absent.** §9.02 expressly contemplates consents in tender/exchange offers; general covenant changes can bind dissenters by majority. However, affected-holder protections include principal, fixed maturity, redemption terms, interest/payment timing, currency, payment suit, adversely changed ranking and specified post-trigger change-of-control obligations. L p.27 L1857 protects changes **“affecting the subordination or ranking of the Notes or the Guarantees”** adverse to holders. A materially adverse ICA modification or unauthorized all/substantially all collateral release needs 66⅔%, not merely majority. §2.09 excludes issuer/control-affiliate votes, with trustee actual-knowledge protection. §6.06 prohibits using the indenture to obtain preference over another holder; §6.07 preserves individual due-payment suits. These are meaningful opposite-case barriers. They do not prove that every non-pro-rata exchange, open-market purchase or asset-based transaction is prohibited, or that a majority can validly override every priority right. No executed coercive LME, uptier or drop-down is established by the supplied actions.

#### Default detail, suspension and funded exit

Payment/nonpayment and notice conditions must not be conflated. Interest gets 30 days (§6.01(a)); principal and due tender payments do not receive that same grace (§6.01(b)). Other covenants require notice from trustee or 25% holders and 60 days, reporting 180 days; §5.01 requires notice but not that passage of time (§6.01(c)). Cross-debt default is a **final-maturity payment failure or acceleration**, not every debt covenant breach; threshold is at least USD50 million and excludes Securitization Entities in that limb. Final/nonappealable judgments use **in excess of** USD50 million and a 60-day unpaid/unstayed test. Collateral failure above USD50 million has a 60-day persistence limb, while a court pleading repudiating validity is separately covered (§6.01(h)). The Company or Significant Subsidiary insolvency events are covered, but automatic acceleration under the controlling §6.02 applies to **Company** bankruptcy events; others use the stated declaration mechanism. Majority can rescind acceleration under conditions and waive nonpayment-excepted past defaults; it cannot simply waive continuing ordinary payment defaults for all dissenters (§§6.02,6.04,6.07).

§4.19 requires both agencies' investment-grade ratings and no continuing Default for suspension. Suspended provisions include RP, upstream restrictions, incurrence, asset sales, affiliates, business, guarantees, preferred stock, impairment and specified merger ratio condition; **liens and change-of-control remain outside that list**. Reversion is prospective, with suspension-period debt allocated under its specified treatment, no retroactive breach solely for suspension-period acts and a 30-day catch-up guarantee accession. Actual suspension notices/ratings are Not supplied; no conclusion that covenants were switched off is made.

Article VIII defeasance is different from ratings suspension: irrevocable USD cash/noncallable government-securities funding sufficient in independent accountants' opinion, tax/legal opinions, officers' certificates, no-default and specified 91-day bankruptcy conditions are required. Covenant defeasance releases the listed covenants including lien/change-of-control obligations and specified cross-default/judgment events; legal defeasance discharges broader obligations with surviving trust-payment rights. Discharge under §8.06 has its own cancellation/due-within-one-year funding conditions. These are not unfunded unilateral covenant escapes, and no exercise is evidenced.
