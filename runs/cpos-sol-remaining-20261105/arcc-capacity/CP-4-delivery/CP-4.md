**Separate remedies and lender control.** Base §5.01(vii), p12/L917, requires coverage **below 100% on the last business day of each of 24 consecutive calendar months**. This is neither the 150% after-borrowing test nor a general two-year cure period. Supplement §4.03, pp2–3/L216–217, separately requires failure to comply with other agreements in the notes/indenture for **60 consecutive calendar days after written notice** from the Trustee or holders of at least **25%** principal. It does not make every external agreement breach subject to that same limb; the distinct borrowed-debt cross-default provision is S §4.04. Under S §4.06, a continuing non-bankruptcy Event of Default permits declaration by Trustee or 25% holders; the specified bankruptcy limbs have automatic consequences. No actual breach, notice, monthly history or acceleration is inferred here.

General amendment authority in B §9.02 is majority-based, with affected-holder exceptions including specified §10.07 changes and payment rights. Past-default waiver exceptions in B §5.13 also matter. Covenant defeasance is permitted for §§10.07–10.08 by S §1.01(g), but requires B §§14.03–14.04 conditions, including sufficient irrevocable trust funding, non-conflict/no-default conditions, tax opinion and certificates. No actual election or satisfaction is supplied. These conditions prevent both extremes: protection cannot be assumed permanent, and a majority cannot simply be assumed able to erase all relevant rights.

The series' change-of-control offer is **100% of principal plus accrued unpaid interest**, not TDG's 101% and not a market price or recovery floor (S pp2–3, §2.01 and Art Six). It requires both defined Change of Control and Below Investment Grade Rating Event: all three agencies below investment grade within the defined window, with attribution requirements. Permitted-holder exclusions matter. Full redemption and a qualifying third-party offer can remove the issuer-offer requirement; timing, lawful-payment, securities-law and Act-related extension conditions apply. This is contingent liquidity protection, not unconditional put capacity today.

### Nearest pressure point

**Pressure Point:** erosion of the selected notes' asset-coverage-based debt-incurrence cushion. **Evidence:** B §10.07 and A65/A274 support published 1.962x against a reported 1.5x after-borrowing minimum. **Risk Mechanic:** portfolio valuation declines reduce the adjusted asset numerator; borrowing adds senior debt and its proceeds/use changes assets; cash-funded distributions reduce assets, while debt-funded distributions can both add debt and remove proceeds. The resulting financing restriction can arrive before a payment default. **Credit Implication:** less room to finance commitments or refinance debt can force asset sales, repayment or equity funding; reduced asset backing also matters to unsecured creditors, without an asserted recovery value. **Monitoring Signal:** next coverage disclosure, pro forma borrowing certificate, portfolio marks, actual draws and executed shareholder returns. **Evidence Needed to Tighten View:** unrounded numerator/denominator, relief bridge, funding uses, facility certificate and accessible-asset schedule. The facility maintenance test receives preference only when near-term breach relevance is evidenced; here its actual headroom is missing, so it cannot displace this quantified incurrence pressure point. No claim is made that this is the group's mathematically tightest covenant.

### Overall capacity view

Overall, Ares Capital Corporation has **limited covenant-capacity visibility**. The main capacity concern is erosion of asset-coverage-based incurrence room, because falling investment values or funded shareholder returns can reduce the ability to raise debt precisely when liquidity is needed. Exact headroom is supported **only at published precision**, not as unrounded certified headroom or actual usable dollars. Further analysis requires the G1–G6 inputs below. The principal documented points are the positive historical cushion; conditional rather than freely available secured financing; unquantified RP/leakage permissions and utilization; and an asset-value definition rather than an EBITDA add-back engine. The single nearest pressure point above governs the capacity challenge, not an unsupported distress narrative.

The strongest contrary case is substantive: reported coverage exceeds the applicable minimum, cash is reported, substantial conditional facility availability is disclosed, and the issuer asserts facility compliance in all material respects at the balance-sheet date. The January 2025 issuance also evidences subsequent capital-market access. That countercase would win against a claim of immediate funding closure if transaction-date certificates show that adequate coverage, borrowing-base eligibility, equity requirements, lender commitments and accessible cash survive the intended uses. The source pack does not establish all those conditions. Accordingly, conviction is higher in the historical ratio comparison than in an available-cash inference. This narrows implementation to documentary follow-up and monitoring; absent price, portfolio mandate and current evidence, it does not support action or sizing.
