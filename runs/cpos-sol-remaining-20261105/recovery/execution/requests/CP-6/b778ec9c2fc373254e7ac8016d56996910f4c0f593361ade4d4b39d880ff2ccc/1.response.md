## Findings

### Committee conclusion

The committee selects **Requires More Work**. Neither side establishes an implementable investment: the Bull supports improving historical operating cash and meaningful contractual protections, while the Bear establishes that actual extraction and conditional legal flexibility can keep that cash from becoming a creditor cushion. The Bear does not prove a liquidity failure, an unlawful payment or an inevitable restructuring. The decisive distinction is between operating capacity and retained, accessible resources. With neither a complete liquidity/downside bridge nor executable prices and portfolio evidence, a Starter Position would convert a plausible credit thesis into unsupported clearance. Avoid would likewise overstate the evidence by treating absent compensation and liquidity proof as demonstrated inadequacy. The conclusion preserves CP-3's Requires More Work/RV Unclear posture and CP-5's Restricted historical-use boundary.

### Committee challenge

The Bull pitched three propositions: improving conversion supports debt service; 2033 guarantees and voting protections materially constrain adverse treatment; historical extensions and delayed term-loan/note bullets provide time to address future funding. The Bear attacked those exact propositions with the documented debt-funded dividend and rising carrying leverage, conditional restricted-payment/investment/release routes, and the earlier receivables-facility renewal requirement. The Bull answered with actual conversion, preserved payment/ranking rights and completed extensions—not hypothetical support or unrestricted cash. The Chair sustained the historical operating evidence, partially mitigated the legal and tenor claims, and rejected the leap from any of them to implementation. This is a genuine challenged long thesis, not an attack on claims that cash was already unrestricted or securities already cheap; no producer made those claims.
