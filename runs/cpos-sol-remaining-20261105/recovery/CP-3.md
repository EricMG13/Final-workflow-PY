### Downside, loss mechanisms and strongest countercases

**Time-aware operating downside:** from the September 2024 base, weaker utilization or qualified-supplier disruption first reduces spares demand or deliverable shipments (CP-1A B10/B11/B14), then revenue/mix and margin (B04/B08), then collections and inventory conversion (B13; CP-1 K03). Interest, reinvestment and committed payouts compete for cash. Borrowing may initially bridge cash needs but raises claims; eligible A/R may tighten just when liquidity is needed. A July 2025 renewal requirement can matter before longer bond maturities, but no outcome is inferred from the missing follow-up. Notes need not suffer an immediate numerical maintenance breach simply because earnings fall. Transaction incurrence/RP permissions, payment default rules and refinancing access are distinct pathways (CP-4 F01/F13).

**Economic loss versus legal loss:** spread widening or rates rising can create mark-to-market loss before any default. Fixed longer notes face duration plus credit-spread repricing; floating loans can have less fixed-rate duration but credit discount and exit-liquidity risk, while higher floating service costs affect issuer cash. In a later restructuring, lower enterprise/pool values, senior dilution, leakage, costs and realization delay reduce distributable proceeds; subordination makes juniors more convex to residual shortfall. A bond's par shortfall is not the investor's entry-price loss: economic loss depends on purchase price, proceeds, interim cash and timing, all of which are missing here. No assumed recovery floor, stress magnitude, PD or portfolio loss is assigned.

**Economic countercase:** installed-base replacement demand, improving historical conversion and completed extensions may allow retained cash to reduce refinancing dependence. It would win if subsequent comparable financial evidence showed recurring conversion after maintenance/integration needs, actual debt retirement and restrained discretionary payouts. That would improve all claims, potentially letting discounted subordinated debt outperform on price; it still needs verified compensation and residual support.

**Legal countercase:** guarantees, intended first-priority liens, junior-or-permitted conditions and sacred voting rights may preserve more reachable value than the leakage-risk narrative suggests.
